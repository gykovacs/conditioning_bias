1.      The method of averaging predictions from different conditioning operators may introduce computational overhead, especially in large-scale applications. While decision trees are relatively fast, the additional prediction time could be a concern in time-sensitive or resource-constrained environments.
2.      The effectiveness of the proposed methods may vary across different types of datasets and features. The evaluation focuses on binary classification and regression tasks, but conditioning bias might behave differently in other types of predictive modeling, such as multi-class classification or time-series forecasting. The author(s) should consider generalization to multi-class classification.
3.      While using both conditioning operators can provide a more comprehensive view, it may also complicate the interpretation of results. Decision trees are valued for their interpretability, and combining results from different conditioning operators could dilute the clarity of the decision-making process.
4.      Although cross-validation is a valuable tool for assessing performance, it cannot fully account for the risks of overfitting associated with conditioning operator selection. The proposal acknowledges this limitation but does not fully address how to mitigate overfitting risks when applying the proposed methods.
5.      The tree mirroring technique and fitting new trees with negated feature values may introduce additional complexity in implementation. For environments where direct access to tree structures is limited, the practical application of these techniques might be challenging, potentially limiting their adoption.

Suggestions:
To address potential computational overhead, the proposal could explore more efficient ways to integrate out the conditioning operator's effect, possibly through algorithmic optimizations or approximations. Expanding the evaluation to include a wider range of predictive tasks and datasets would provide a more comprehensive understanding of the methods' effectiveness and limitations. Offering strategies for maintaining interpretability while using multiple conditioning operators could enhance the practical utility of the proposed methods.
