The paper analyzes the bias that is created in decision trees by the design decision of using a "greater or equal" than or "greater than" operator. The choice of one or the other operator can change the decision of an example when the threshold is an existing value. This can occur specially for discrete attributes. The paper shows that this bias happens more than one would expect. In addition, the paper shows that using one operator or the other could affect with statistical significance the performance of the decision tree or random forest. Then the paper also proposes a way to avoid the bias by averaging two equal, if possible, trees (one this the <= operator and the other with <). The results show that the proposed method is never significantly worse than both operators when used individually and it is more times significantly better than worse.

The paper is very well posed for an idea that seems marginal although the paper shows that the proposed idea reduces the risk for _free_. In any case, the differences are small.
